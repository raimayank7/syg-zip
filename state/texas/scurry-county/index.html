Where Scurry County is

The ZIP codes of Scurry County are found in the north of Texas. Its codes spread over about 20 miles from north to south and 20 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 79517 (Fluvanna) and 79526 (Hermleigh), about 25 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Mitchell County (20 miles), Borden County (31 miles), Fisher County (35 miles) and Garza County (37 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Scurry County starts with 795. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 795 is shared with 13 other counties, such as Callahan County, Coleman County and Fisher County, and has 49 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 4 of the 6 codes. Together they come to about 1,318 square miles of land (3,413 sq km). The largest is 79549 (Snyder) with 860 square miles, and the smallest is 79527 (Ira) with 61.6. The middle-sized code here covers 198 square miles; the middle value for all of Texas is 47.8. Large code areas usually go with open country, where one post office serves a wide district. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Scurry County; a code can reach across its border. 2 codes have no mapped area: 79516 (Dunn) and 79550 (Snyder). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Scurry County ranks 101st among the 254 counties and county-level areas of Texas (equal counts share a rank), with 6 of the 2,600 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Scurry County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters TX and the ZIP code:

  • Snyder, TX 79549 (one of 2 codes for Snyder; the street decides which)
  • Dunn, TX 79516
  • Fluvanna, TX 79517

Snyder is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 3 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
