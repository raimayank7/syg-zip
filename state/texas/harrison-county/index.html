Where Harrison County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Harrison County is in the north-east of Texas. Its codes spread over about 20 miles from north to south and 25 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 75642 (Elysian Fields) and 75651 (Harleton), about 26 miles from each other in a straight line. The nearest neighbours on the ZIP code map, measured between the middle points of their codes, are Marion County (20 miles), Panola County (26 miles), Gregg County (29 miles) and Caddo Parish, Louisiana (29 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Harrison County starts with 756. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 756 is shared with 8 other counties, such as Camp County, Cass County and Gregg County, and has 57 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 10 of the 11 codes. Together they come to about 768 square miles of land (1,990 sq km). The largest is 75672 (Marshall) with 232 square miles, and the smallest is 75694 (Woodlawn) with 0.07. The middle-sized code here covers 77.3 square miles; the middle value for all of Texas is 47.8. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Harrison County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 75688 (Scottsville). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Harrison County ranks 46th among the 254 counties and county-level areas of Texas (equal counts share a rank), with 11 of the 2,600 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Harrison County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters TX and the ZIP code:

  • Marshall, TX 75670 (one of 3 codes for Marshall; the street decides which)
  • Elysian Fields, TX 75642
  • Hallsville, TX 75650

Marshall is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 14 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
