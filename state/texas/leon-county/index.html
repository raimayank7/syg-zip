Where Leon County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Leon County is in the east of Texas. Its codes spread over about 36 miles from north to south and 23 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 75855 (Oakwood) and 77871 (Normangee), about 39 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Madison County (24 miles), Freestone County (33 miles), Robertson County (38 miles) and Limestone County (39 miles).

The first three digits

The codes of Leon County begin with 758 (5 codes) and 778 (4 codes). The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 758 is shared with 5 other counties, such as Anderson County, Freestone County and Houston County, and has 33 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 8 of the 9 codes. Together they come to about 1,375 square miles of land (3,562 sq km). The largest is 75855 (Oakwood) with 285 square miles, and the smallest is 77855 (Flynn) with 13.7. The middle-sized code here covers 181 square miles; the middle value for all of Texas is 47.8. Large code areas usually go with open country, where one post office serves a wide district. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Leon County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 77850 (Concord). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Leon County ranks 61st among the 254 counties and county-level areas of Texas (equal counts share a rank), with 9 of the 2,600 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Leon County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters TX and the ZIP code:

  • Buffalo, TX 75831
  • Centerville, TX 75833
  • Concord, TX 77850

Buffalo is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 16 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
