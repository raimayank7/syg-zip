Where Brown County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Brown County sits near the middle of Texas. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 30 miles, and from east to west about 20 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 76827 (Brookesmith) and 76857 (May), about 32 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Comanche County (30 miles), Coleman County (31 miles), Mills County (31 miles) and Eastland County (41 miles).

The first three digits

The codes of Brown County begin with 768 (8 codes) and 764 (1 code). The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 768 is shared with 13 other counties, such as Coleman County, Concho County and Edwards County, and has 53 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 7 of the 9 codes. Together they come to about 973 square miles of land (2,520 sq km). The largest is 76801 (Brownwood) with 376 square miles, and the smallest is 76827 (Brookesmith) with 81.8. The middle-sized code here covers 101 square miles; the middle value for all of Texas is 47.8. Large code areas usually go with open country, where one post office serves a wide district. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Brown County; a code can reach across its border. 2 codes have no mapped area: 76803 (Early) and 76804 (Brownwood). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Brown County ranks 61st among the 254 counties and county-level areas of Texas (equal counts share a rank), with 9 of the 2,600 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Brown County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters TX and the ZIP code:
