Washington ZIP codes in brief

This list holds 716 ZIP codes for Washington, about 1.7% of the 40,977 on this site, which puts it 25th among the 51 state lists by size. They are spread over 39 counties and county-level areas. The most codes are in King County (120), Pierce County (79), Spokane County (53), Snohomish County (37) and Kitsap County (26). At the other end, 2 have only one or two codes, for example Columbia County and Garfield County.

Prefixes and first digits

The first three digits of a ZIP code name a mail sorting area. Washington uses 14 of these prefixes, from 980 to 994. The busiest are 983 (81 codes, most of them for Bremerton), 985 (77 codes, most of them for Olympia), 982 (76 codes, most of them for Everett) and 980 (71 codes, most of them for Bellevue). The first digit 9 is shared with Alaska, California, Hawaii and Oregon.

Largest, smallest and farthest

The Census Bureau draws an area for 605 of the 716 codes. The largest is 98826 (Leavenworth, Chelan County) at 1,016 square miles and the smallest is 98154 (Seattle) at under 0.01; the middle value is 35.1 square miles. The other 111 have no mapped area and are normally codes for post office boxes or single buildings. By map point, the northernmost code is 98231 (Blaine) and the southernmost 98671 (Washougal); the one farthest east is 99402 (Asotin) and farthest west 98350 (La Push).

Place names and addresses in Washington

Of the 491 place names in the list, 451 have a single ZIP code. The longest lists belong to Seattle (59), Tacoma (35), Spokane (28) and Vancouver (14). 2 place names appear under more than one county, among them Bothell and Woodinville: there the ZIP code, not the name, tells you which is meant. In an address, write the city, a comma, the letters WA and the code, as in Seattle, WA 98101. The name of the county is left out.
