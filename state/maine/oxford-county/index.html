Where Oxford County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Oxford County is in the south-west of Maine. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 60 miles, and from east to west about 35 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 04068 (Porter) and 04261 (Newry), about 60 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Androscoggin County (24 miles), Franklin County (37 miles), Coos County, New Hampshire (40 miles) and Cumberland County (41 miles).

The first three digits

The codes of Oxford County begin with 042 (24 codes) and 040 (8 codes). The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 042 is shared with 5 other counties, such as Androscoggin County, Cumberland County and Franklin County, and has 60 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 31 of the 32 codes. Together they come to about 1,686 square miles of land (4,368 sq km). The largest is 04261 (Newry) with 208 square miles, and the smallest is 04286 (West Bethel) with 0.01. The middle-sized code here covers 43.8 square miles; the middle value for all of Maine is 36.7. Water adds about 53.6 square miles, roughly 3% of the mapped area. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Oxford County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 04016 (Center Lovell). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Oxford County ranks 7th among the 16 counties and county-level areas of Maine (equal counts share a rank), with 32 of the 485 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Oxford County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters ME and the ZIP code:

  • Andover, ME 04216
  • Bethel, ME 04217
  • Brownfield, ME 04010

Andover is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 12 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
