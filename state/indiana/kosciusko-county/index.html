Where Kosciusko County is

The ZIP codes of Kosciusko County are found in the north-east of Indiana. Its codes spread over about 23 miles from north to south and 17 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 46567 (Syracuse) and 46982 (Silver Lake), about 24 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Whitley County (20 miles), Marshall County (23 miles), Elkhart County (25 miles) and Noble County (25 miles).

The first three digits

The codes of Kosciusko County begin with 465 (14 codes) and 469 (1 code). The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 465 is shared with 5 other counties, such as Elkhart County, LaGrange County and Marshall County, and has 52 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 14 of the 15 codes. Together they come to about 523 square miles of land (1,356 sq km). The largest is 46580 (Warsaw) with 71.1 square miles, and the smallest is 46502 (Atwood) with 0.02. The middle-sized code here covers 39.7 square miles; the middle value for all of Indiana is 32.6. Water adds about 22.7 square miles, roughly 4% of the mapped area. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Kosciusko County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 46581 (Warsaw). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Kosciusko County ranks 11th among the 92 counties and county-level areas of Indiana (equal counts share a rank), with 15 of the 968 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Kosciusko County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters IN and the ZIP code:

  • Warsaw, IN 46580 (one of 3 codes for Warsaw; the street decides which)
  • Atwood, IN 46502
  • Burket, IN 46508

Warsaw is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 8 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
