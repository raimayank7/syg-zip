Where Jones County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Jones County is in the south-east of Mississippi. Its codes spread over about 23 miles from north to south and 16 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 39436 (Eastabuchie) and 39477 (Sandersville), about 28 miles from each other in a straight line. The nearest neighbours on the ZIP code map, measured between the middle points of their codes, are Jasper County (21 miles), Covington County (23 miles), Smith County (26 miles) and Perry County (27 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Jones County starts with 394. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 394 is shared with 10 other counties, such as Covington County, Forrest County and George County, and has 45 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 6 of the 9 codes. Together they come to about 596 square miles of land (1,544 sq km). The largest is 39443 (Laurel) with 292 square miles, and the smallest is 39477 (Sandersville) with 5.2. The middle-sized code here covers 55.8 square miles; the middle value for all of Mississippi is 83.6. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Jones County; a code can reach across its border. 3 codes have no mapped area: 39436 (Eastabuchie), 39441 (Laurel) and 39442 (Laurel). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Jones County ranks 13th among the 82 counties and county-level areas of Mississippi (equal counts share a rank), with 9 of the 531 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Jones County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters MS and the ZIP code:

  • Laurel, MS 39440 (one of 4 codes for Laurel; the street decides which)
  • Eastabuchie, MS 39436
  • Ellisville, MS 39437

Laurel is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 8 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
