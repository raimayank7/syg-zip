Where Carroll County is

On the map, the ZIP codes of Carroll County fall in the north-west of Georgia. Its codes spread over about 23 miles from north to south and 19 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 30170 (Roopville) and 30179 (Temple), about 25 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Haralson County (14 miles), Douglas County (21 miles), Cleburne County, Alabama (22 miles) and Heard County (23 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Carroll County starts with 301. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 301 is shared with 10 other counties, such as Bartow County, Cherokee County and Cobb County, and has 77 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 8 of the 12 codes. Together they come to about 561 square miles of land (1,453 sq km). The largest is 30117 (Carrollton) with 108 square miles, and the smallest is 30118 (Carrollton) with 0.2. The middle-sized code here covers 75.4 square miles; the middle value for all of Georgia is 53.5. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Carroll County; a code can reach across its border. 4 codes have no mapped area: 30109 (Bowdon Junction), 30112 (Carrollton), 30119 (Carrollton) and 30150 (Mount Zion). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Carroll County ranks 10th among the 157 counties and county-level areas of Georgia (equal counts share a rank), with 12 of the 952 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Carroll County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters GA and the ZIP code:

  • Carrollton, GA 30112 (one of 5 codes for Carrollton; the street decides which)
  • Bowdon, GA 30108
  • Bowdon Junction, GA 30109

Carrollton is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 9 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
