Where Orleans County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Orleans County is in the north of Vermont. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 32 miles, and from east to west about 23 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 05823 (Beebe Plain) and 05842 (Greensboro Bend), about 32 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Lamoille County (25 miles), Essex County (26 miles), Caledonia County (27 miles) and Franklin County (34 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Orleans County starts with 058. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 058 is shared with 2 other counties, Caledonia County and Essex County, and has 46 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 22 of the 23 codes. Together they come to about 691 square miles of land (1,789 sq km). The largest is 05860 (Orleans) with 76.7 square miles, and the smallest is 05825 (Coventry) with 1.0. The middle-sized code here covers 29.0 square miles; the middle value for all of Vermont is 31.4. Water adds about 27.5 square miles, roughly 4% of the mapped area. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Orleans County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 05823 (Beebe Plain). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Orleans County ranks 7th among the 14 counties and county-level areas of Vermont (equal counts share a rank), with 23 of the 308 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Orleans County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters VT and the ZIP code:

  • Barton, VT 05822 (one of 2 codes for Barton; the street decides which)
  • Albany, VT 05820
  • Beebe Plain, VT 05823

Barton is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 4 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
