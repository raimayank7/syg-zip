Vermont ZIP codes in brief

This list holds 308 ZIP codes for Vermont, about 0.8% of the 40,977 on this site, which puts it 43rd among the 51 state lists by size. They are spread over 14 counties and county-level areas. The most codes are in Windsor County (37), Rutland County (30), Washington County (30), Windham County (30) and Chittenden County (26). The fewest are in Grand Isle County, with 5.

Prefixes and first digits

The first three digits of a ZIP code name a mail sorting area. Vermont uses 9 of these prefixes, from 050 to 059. The busiest are 054 (60 codes, most of them for Burlington), 050 (54 codes, most of them for White River Junction), 058 (46 codes, most of them for Barton) and 057 (45 codes, most of them for Rutland). The first digit 0 is shared with Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York and Rhode Island.

Largest, smallest and farthest

The Census Bureau draws an area for 265 of the 308 codes. The largest is 05905 (Guildhall, Essex County) at 186 square miles and the smallest is 05481 (Saint Albans Bay) at 0.01; the middle value is 31.4 square miles. The other 43 have no mapped area and are normally codes for post office boxes or single buildings. By map point, the northernmost code is 05902 (Beecher Falls) and the southernmost 05352 (Stamford); the one farthest east is 05902 (Beecher Falls) and farthest west 05734 (Bridport).

Place names and addresses in Vermont

Of the 286 place names in the list, 275 have a single ZIP code. The longest lists belong to Montpelier (7), Burlington (5), Brattleboro (4) and Colchester (3). In an address, write the city, a comma, the letters VT and the code, as in Montpelier, VT 05601. The name of the county is left out.
