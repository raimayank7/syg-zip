Where Miami County is

On the map, the ZIP codes of Miami County fall in the west of Ohio. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 14 miles, and from east to west about 21 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 45317 (Conover) and 45361 (Potsdam), about 24 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Darke County (20 miles), Shelby County (21 miles), Montgomery County (21 miles) and Clark County (24 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Miami County starts with 453. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 453 is shared with 8 other counties, such as Champaign County, Clark County and Darke County, and has 80 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 13 of the 14 codes. Together they come to about 457 square miles of land (1,183 sq km). The largest is 45373 (Troy) with 87.7 square miles, and the smallest is 45361 (Potsdam) with 0.4. The middle-sized code here covers 23.5 square miles; the middle value for all of Ohio is 22.2. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Miami County; a code can reach across its border. One code has no mapped area: 45374 (Troy). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Miami County ranks 30th among the 88 counties and county-level areas of Ohio (equal counts share a rank), with 14 of the 1,416 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Miami County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters OH and the ZIP code:

  • Troy, OH 45373 (one of 2 codes for Troy; the street decides which)
  • Bradford, OH 45308
  • Casstown, OH 45312

Troy is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 19 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.

Which ZIP code in Miami County covers the most land?
