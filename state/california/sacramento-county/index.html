Where Sacramento County is

On the map, the ZIP codes of Sacramento County fall in the north-west of California. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 39 miles, and from east to west about 28 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 95641 (Isleton) and 95671 (Represa), about 45 miles from each other in a straight line. The codes of Sacramento County begin with 942 (59 codes), 958 (42 codes), 956 (25 codes) and 957 (6 codes). The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. No other county-level area in this list uses 942, so those three digits alone point to Sacramento County.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 55 of the 132 codes. Together they come to about 1,016 square miles of land (2,632 sq km). The largest is 95632 (Galt) with 113 square miles, and the smallest is 95814 (Sacramento) with 1.4. The middle-sized code here covers 8.9 square miles; the middle value for all of California is 15.2. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Sacramento County; a code can reach across its border. 77 codes have no mapped area: 94203 (Sacramento), 94204 (Sacramento), 94205 (Sacramento), 94206 (Sacramento), 94207 (Sacramento) and 72 more. Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Sacramento County ranks 4th among the 58 counties and county-level areas of California (equal counts share a rank), with 132 of the 2,593 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Sacramento County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters CA and the ZIP code:

  • Sacramento, CA 94203 (one of 100 codes for Sacramento; the street decides which)
  • Elk Grove, CA 95624 (one of 4 codes for Elk Grove; the street decides which)
  • Citrus Heights, CA 95610 (one of 3 codes for Citrus Heights; the street decides which)

Sacramento is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 3 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
