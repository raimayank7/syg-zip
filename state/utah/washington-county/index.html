Where Washington County is

Going by the map points of its ZIP codes, Washington County is in the south-west of Utah. Its codes spread over about 40 miles from north to south and 43 miles from east to west. The two codes farthest apart are 84725 (Enterprise) and 84784 (Hildale), about 56 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Iron County (39 miles), Kane County (47 miles), Lincoln County, Nevada (78 miles) and Beaver County (80 miles).

The first three digits

Every ZIP code of Washington County starts with 847. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 847 is shared with 8 other counties, such as Beaver County, Garfield County and Iron County, and has 71 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 20 of the 22 codes. Together they come to about 1,551 square miles of land (4,016 sq km). The largest is 84737 (Hurricane) with 250 square miles, and the smallest is 84784 (Hildale) with 4.2. The middle-sized code here covers 51.1 square miles; the middle value for all of Utah is 49.9. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Washington County; a code can reach across its border. 2 codes have no mapped area: 84771 (Saint George) and 84791 (Saint George). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Washington County ranks 3rd among the 29 counties and county-level areas of Utah (equal counts share a rank), with 22 of the 347 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Washington County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters UT and the ZIP code:

  • Saint George, UT 84770 (one of 4 codes for Saint George; the street decides which)
  • Central, UT 84722
  • Dammeron Valley, UT 84783

Saint George is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 3 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
