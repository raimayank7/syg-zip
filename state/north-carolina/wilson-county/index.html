Where Wilson County is

On the map, the ZIP codes of Wilson County fall in the north-east of North Carolina. From the northernmost code to the southernmost is about 15 miles, and from east to west about 17 miles. The two codes farthest apart are 27873 (Saratoga) and 27880 (Sims), about 18 miles from each other in a straight line. Measured between the middle points of their codes, the closest neighbours are Nash County (18 miles), Edgecombe County (21 miles), Greene County (22 miles) and Johnston County (24 miles).

The first three digits

All 10 codes here begin with the same three digits, 278. The first three digits of a ZIP code name the mail sorting area it belongs to. The prefix 278 is shared with 12 other counties, such as Beaufort County, Bertie County and Edgecombe County, and has 93 codes in all.

How much ground the codes cover

The Census Bureau draws an area for 8 of the 10 codes. Together they come to about 382 square miles of land (990 sq km). The largest is 27893 (Wilson) with 109 square miles, and the smallest is 27873 (Saratoga) with 0.6. The middle-sized code here covers 39.7 square miles; the middle value for all of North Carolina is 42.4. These areas follow ZIP code delivery routes, so their total is not the official size of Wilson County; a code can reach across its border. 2 codes have no mapped area: 27894 (Wilson) and 27895 (Wilson). Codes like these are normally for post office boxes or one large building.

By number of ZIP codes, Wilson County ranks 36th among the 100 counties and county-level areas of North Carolina (equal counts share a rank), with 10 of the 1,081 codes in the state list.

Writing an address in Wilson County

The name of the county is not written in a United States address. The last line is the postal city, the letters NC and the ZIP code:

  • Wilson, NC 27893 (one of 4 codes for Wilson; the street decides which)
  • Black Creek, NC 27813
  • Elm City, NC 27822

Wilson is also the name of a place with a ZIP code in 9 other states, which is why the two state letters and the code matter more than the town name.
